Power Semigroups

Power semigroups arise from a very simple idea: instead of studying the elements of a (multiplicatively written) semigroup \(S\) one by one, we consider sets of elements and multiply them in a natural way. More precisely, we can define a multiplication on the power set of \(S\) by setting

\[ X \cdot Y := \{ xy : x \in X,\; y \in Y \}, \quad \text{for all } X, Y \subseteq S. \]

With this operation, suitable families of subsets of \(S\) themselves form semigroups. These are generically called power semigroups of \(S\). Most notably, the family of all non-empty subsets of $S$ forms, under the above operation of setwise multiplication, the large power semigroup of \(S\), whereas the family of all non-empty finite subsets of \(S\) forms the finitary power semigroup of \(S\).

These constructions are extremely natural and appear in several areas of mathematics and theoretical computer science. In particular, power semigroups play a role in the theory of formal languages and automata, and they have been studied from different algebraic perspectives, including semigroup theory, universal algebra, and more recently factorization theory and arithmetic combinatorics.

Historically, explicit appearances of power semigroups can be traced back at least to a 1950 paper by Ballieu. A systematic investigation began in the late 1960s and developed extensively in the following decades, with many contributions throughout the 1980s and 1990s.

The purpose of this page is to provide a centralized resource on power semigroups, including background material, historical notes, open problems, and an annotated bibliography of the literature on the subject.
